## Market overview

With 34,000,000 verified contacts, the Iran Consumer Email List is one of LeadsBlue's consumer email list datasets for Iran. Iran has a GDP of approximately $400 billion at purchasing power parity, making it one of the largest economies in the Middle East. However, its actual market access for international B2B vendors is severely constrained by comprehensive US, EU, and UN sanctions. The economy is dominated by oil and gas accounting for over 20% of GDP, followed by petrochemicals, automotive manufacturing, steel, and agriculture. Iran has a population of 87 million with a large, educated professional class and significant domestic industrial capacity. Tehran is the commercial capital with a dense concentration of manufacturing, financial, and state-owned enterprises.

For international B2B vendors, Iran represents one of the least accessible markets globally. The sanctions regime prohibits most financial transactions, blocks SWIFT connectivity for Iranian banks, and prevents the provision of most technology and services by US and EU companies. Business culture in Iran is highly relationship-dependent, hierarchical, and oriented around long-term trust. Farsi (Persian) is the exclusive business language — English is used by some educated professionals but is not standard in formal business communication. The work week runs Sunday to Thursday. B2B email marketing from international vendors to Iran is practically non-functional due to sanctions, internet restrictions, national email filtering, and the inability to process payments or contracts under most international jurisdictions.

## Outreach strategy

B2B cold email to Iran from US or EU-based vendors is not recommended and may be non-compliant with sanctions regulations. Outreach that results in service provision, payment processing, or technology transfer to Iranian entities can trigger sanctions violations regardless of email intent. For vendors in jurisdictions without Iran sanctions exposure, outreach should be exclusively in Farsi, formal in tone, and relationship-focused rather than transactional. If outreach is legally permissible, optimal timing follows the Sunday-to-Thursday work week, with Tuesday and Wednesday being highest-engagement days. Avoid outreach during Ramadan, which causes a 40 to 60 percent engagement drop, and during the Nowruz Iranian New Year period in late March. The Iranian internet is heavily filtered — standard email deliverability to Iranian corporate inboxes from foreign servers is significantly impaired by national firewall restrictions.

## How to use this list effectively

- **Verify before sending.** B2B contact data decays roughly 2–3% per month as people change roles. Run the list through a verification pass to remove stale or invalid addresses before your first send.
- **Segment by industry and role.** Response rates improve sharply when the message matches the recipient's vertical and seniority — use the segment fields to tailor your copy rather than sending one generic message.
- **Warm up the sending domain.** Start with low daily volume from a dedicated sending domain and ramp gradually to protect deliverability.
- **Follow a multi-touch cadence.** Most replies come from the 2nd–4th touch; a short sequence over 2–3 weeks outperforms a single send.
- **Respect compliance.** Include a valid postal address and a working opt-out, and honour unsubscribe requests promptly (see compliance notes above).
